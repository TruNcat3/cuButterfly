\begin{abstract}
Butterfly computation underlies Fourier, number-theoretic, Hadamard, and zeta
transforms. Their shared dependencies expose a common performance problem.
Spatial expansion consumes execution resources and live storage, while
temporal reuse requires state retention or repeated communication. Their
balance depends on the arithmetic, workload, and hardware. We characterize
these demands jointly so that organization can be optimized under a target's
capacity, bandwidth, and execution constraints.

We present \system{}, which derives dependency-closed local regions, their
communication and readiness relations, and resource demands from butterfly
dependencies. \emph{Hierarchical hybrid
dataflow} combines spatial expansion and temporal reuse along stage and data
dimensions, repeating the construction across storage scopes. The resulting organization method reuses these relations across arithmetic
cores and workloads while evaluating demand against target hardware constraints.

An A100 80GB evaluation observes geometric-mean latency ratios
of \CurrentFFTSingle$\times$ and \CurrentFFTDouble$\times$ over cuFFT
for its FP32 and FP64 FFT cohorts, including multidimensional requests.
An independent cyclic NTT study reaches \CurrentCyclicNTT$\times$ over
GPU-NTT across 54 matched 32/64-bit-word requests.
Separate fixed-core controls improve complete-call performance by approximately
1.22$\times$ with either FFT leaf, with local organization gains also observed for
FWHT and NTT. These measurements support reusable organization on the tested
workloads. Selected CKKS complete operations improve by \CurrentCKKSFast$\times$
over the faster of two native execution choices across 36 short-chain requests.
Fixed-core controls distinguish organization gains from core substitution,
while FP16 and application losses expose limits. Floating-point errors are
reported without a fixed acceptance threshold.
\end{abstract}
