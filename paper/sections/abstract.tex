\begin{abstract}
Butterfly transforms support scientific computing, machine learning, and
cryptography. Efficient execution depends on how their changing pair
dependencies are organized under finite storage, bandwidth, and compute
resources. Even with a fixed arithmetic core, more local reuse can improve
or degrade performance. This recurring trade-off motivates a common method
that retains specialized arithmetic while adapting execution organization.

We present \system{}, which derives local regions, producer--consumer
intersections, and readiness from labeled butterfly dependencies. These
relations connect spatial expansion and temporal reuse to resource demand.
\emph{Hierarchical hybrid dataflow} repeats the construction across storage
scopes, composing local residency and boundary exchange under hardware
constraints. The construction is shared across arithmetic and workload
variants, while their feasible and beneficial organizations can differ.

On A100, fixed-core controls obtain approximately 1.22$\times$ organization
gains with either of two FFT leaves, with conditional gains also observed
for FWHT and NTT. Separate complete-call comparisons obtain geometric means
of \CurrentFFTSingle$\times$/\CurrentFFTDouble$\times$ over cuFFT for FP32/FP64,
including adapted and multidimensional requests, and \CurrentCyclicNTT$\times$
over GPU-NTT across 54 matched requests. Thirty-six short-chain CKKS requests
achieve \CurrentCKKSFast$\times$ over the faster of two native execution choices.
FP16 and application losses delimit these benefits. Floating-point errors are
reported without a fixed acceptance threshold, and cross-architecture
validation remains open.
\end{abstract}
