\section{Conclusion}
\system{} derives local regions, communication and readiness relations, and
resource demands from butterfly dependencies. Hierarchical hybrid organization
reuses this construction across arithmetic cores and workloads while evaluating
its demands under target hardware constraints. Fixed-core experiments isolate
organization gains; library and application comparisons expose both benefits
and losses. The evidence supports a reusable organization method on the tested
A100 workloads and numerical contracts, rather than a universal mapping or
hardware optimum.
